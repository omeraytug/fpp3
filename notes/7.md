# Chapter 7 - Regression Models

## Main Idea

Regression models forecast a target variable using one or more predictor variables.

General form:

$$
y_t = \beta_0 + \beta_1 x_{1,t} + \beta_2 x_{2,t} + \cdots + \beta_k x_{k,t} + \varepsilon_t
$$

where:

- y = forecast variable (target)
- x = predictor variables (features)
- β = coefficients
- ε = random error

---

# 7.1 Linear Regression

## Simple Regression

One predictor:

$$
y_t = \beta_0 + \beta_1 x_t + \varepsilon_t
$$

### Interpretation

Intercept:

$$
\beta_0
$$

Predicted value when x = 0.

Slope:

$$
\beta_1
$$

Average change in y from a one-unit increase in x.

---

## Multiple Regression

Multiple predictors:

$$
y_t =
\beta_0 +
\beta_1 x_{1,t} +
\beta_2 x_{2,t} +
\cdots +
\beta_k x_{k,t}
+
\varepsilon_t
$$

Each coefficient measures the effect of a predictor while holding all other predictors fixed.

---

# Regression Assumptions

The model assumes:

1. Linear relationship.
2. Errors have mean zero.

$$
E(\varepsilon)=0
$$

3. Errors are not autocorrelated.
4. Errors are unrelated to predictors.
5. Constant variance (homoscedasticity).
6. Normal errors (mainly for prediction intervals).

---

# 7.2 Least Squares

Goal:

Choose coefficients that minimize:

$$
SSE=\sum e_t^2
$$

where

$$
e_t=y_t-\hat y_t
$$

This is called Least Squares Estimation.

---

## Fitted Values

Predictions inside training data:

$$
\hat y_t =
\hat\beta_0 +
\hat\beta_1 x_{1,t}
+
\cdots
+
\hat\beta_k x_{k,t}
$$

Important:

- Fitted values = predictions on training data.
- Forecasts = predictions for future data.

---

# Goodness of Fit

## R²

Measures proportion of variance explained.

$$
0 \le R^2 \le 1
$$

Interpretation:

- 0 = explains nothing
- 1 = perfect fit

Higher is better.

---

## Problems with R²

Adding predictors almost always increases R².

Therefore:

Do NOT use R² alone for model selection.

---

## Residual Standard Error

Measures typical size of residuals.

Lower is better.

$$
\hat\sigma_e
=
\sqrt{
\frac{\sum e_t^2}
{T-k-1}
}
$$

---

# 7.3 Residual Diagnostics

Residual:

$$
e_t=y_t-\hat y_t
$$

Residuals should look like white noise.

---

## Check 1: Residual Time Plot

Want:

- Random pattern
- No trend
- No seasonality

---

## Check 2: ACF Plot

Want:

- No significant autocorrelation

If residuals are autocorrelated:

Model is missing information.

---

## Check 3: Histogram

Want:

- Approximately normal distribution

Important for prediction intervals.

---

## Check 4: Residuals vs Predictors

Want:

- Random scatter

Pattern indicates:

- Missing predictor
- Nonlinear relationship

---

## Check 5: Residuals vs Fitted Values

Want:

- Constant spread

Funnel shape indicates:

- Heteroscedasticity

---

# Outliers

Outlier:

Extreme observation.

Influential Observation:

Observation that changes coefficient estimates substantially.

Always investigate before removing.

---

# Spurious Regression

Occurs when two unrelated trending series appear related.

Warning signs:

- Very high R²
- Strong residual autocorrelation

Example:

Australian passengers vs Guinea rice production.

Looks related.

Actually unrelated.

---

# 7.4 Useful Predictors

## Trend

Add time as predictor:

$$
x_t=t
$$

Model:

$$
y_t=\beta_0+\beta_1 t+\varepsilon_t
$$

---

## Dummy Variables

Used for categorical predictors.

Example:

Holiday

| Holiday | Dummy |
|----------|--------|
| Yes | 1 |
| No | 0 |

---

## Seasonal Dummies

For m seasons:

Use:

$$
m-1
$$

dummy variables.

Examples:

- Quarterly → 3 dummies
- Monthly → 11 dummies
- Weekly → 6 dummies

---

## Dummy Variable Trap

Never use all categories.

One category must be omitted.

Otherwise perfect multicollinearity occurs.

---

## Intervention Variables

### Spike

One-time event.

0 0 1 0 0

---

### Step

Permanent level shift.

0 0 0 1 1 1 1

---

## Trading Day Variables

Can include:

- Number of Mondays
- Number of Tuesdays
- etc.

Useful for retail sales.

---

## Distributed Lags

Include lagged predictors.

Example:

$$
x_t
$$

$$
x_{t-1}
$$

$$
x_{t-2}
$$

Useful for advertising effects.

---

# Fourier Terms

Alternative to seasonal dummies.

Use:

$$
\sin
\left(
\frac{2\pi t}{m}
\right)
$$

$$
\cos
\left(
\frac{2\pi t}{m}
\right)
$$

Advantages:

- Fewer predictors
- Good for long seasonality

Example:

Weekly seasonality:

$$
m=52
$$

---

# 7.5 Selecting Predictors

Avoid:

- Looking only at scatterplots
- Keeping variables solely based on p-values

---

## Adjusted R²

Corrects R² for number of predictors.

Higher is better.

---

## Cross Validation (CV)

Measures predictive performance.

Lower is better.

---

## AIC

Balances:

- Fit
- Model complexity

Lower is better.

---

## AICc

Corrected AIC.

Preferred in FPP3.

Lower is better.

---

## BIC

Stronger penalty than AIC.

Usually chooses smaller models.

Lower is better.

---

## Model Selection Rule

FPP3 recommendation:

1. AICc
2. AIC
3. CV

Use the model with the smallest value.

---

## Stepwise Regression

### Backward

Start with all predictors.

Remove one at a time.

---

### Forward

Start with intercept only.

Add predictors one at a time.

---

# 7.6 Forecasting with Regression

## Ex-Ante Forecast

Future predictors are unknown.

Need forecasts of predictors.

True forecast.

---

## Ex-Post Forecast

Future predictor values are known.

Used for evaluation.

Not a real forecast.

---

## Scenario Forecasting

Assume future predictor values.

Example:

Scenario 1:

- Income = +1%

Scenario 2:

- Income = -1%

Compare forecasts.

---

# Prediction Intervals

Wider when:

- Residual variance is large
- Predictor is far from historical average

Always remember:

Prediction intervals from regression assume predictor values are known.

---

# 7.7 Nonlinear Regression

Used when straight lines are inadequate.

---

## Log-Log Model

$$
\log(y)
=
\beta_0
+
\beta_1\log(x)
+
\varepsilon
$$

Interpretation:

$$
\beta_1
$$

is elasticity.

1% increase in x produces approximately β₁% increase in y.

---

## Log-Linear Model

$$
\log(y)
=
\beta_0
+
\beta_1 x
+
\varepsilon
$$

Creates exponential trend.

---

## Linear-Log Model

$$
y
=
\beta_0
+
\beta_1 \log(x)
+
\varepsilon
$$

Diminishing effect of x.

---

## Handling Zeros

Use:

$$
\log(x+1)
$$

instead of:

$$
\log(x)
$$

---

# Piecewise Linear Regression

Allows slope changes.

Knot:

Point where slope changes.

Create:

$$
(x-c)^+
=
\max(0,x-c)
$$

---

## Piecewise Trend

Use:

$$
t
$$

and

$$
(t-\tau)^+
$$

Before knot:

$$
Slope=\beta_1
$$

After knot:

$$
Slope=\beta_1+\beta_2
$$

---

# Regression Splines

Multiple knots:

$$
(x-c_1)^+
$$

$$
(x-c_2)^+
$$

$$
(x-c_3)^+
$$

Allow multiple bends.

---

# 7.8 Correlation vs Causation

Correlation does NOT imply causation.

A predictor can improve forecasts even if it does not cause the target.

Forecasting cares about prediction, not causality.

---

# Confounding

A third variable affects both x and y.

Example:

Temperature affects:

- Ice cream sales
- Drownings

---

# Multicollinearity

Occurs when predictors contain similar information.

Example:

- Left foot size
- Right foot size

---

## Problems

- Coefficients become unstable
- p-values become unreliable

---

## Forecasting Impact

Usually not a major issue if:

- Good software is used
- Future predictors stay within historical ranges

---

# Key Exam Points

- Regression predicts y using x variables.
- Coefficients estimated using least squares.
- Residuals should resemble white noise.
- Trend = time as predictor.
- Use m-1 seasonal dummies.
- Avoid dummy variable trap.
- Fourier terms are alternatives to seasonal dummies.
- AICc is usually preferred for model selection.
- Ex-ante forecasts require predictor forecasts.
- Log-log coefficient = elasticity.
- Knots create piecewise trends.
- Correlation does not imply causation.
- High R² does not guarantee a good forecasting model.
